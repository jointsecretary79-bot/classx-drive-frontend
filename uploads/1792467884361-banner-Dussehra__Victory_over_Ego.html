दशहरा
Victory over Ego
Jai Shri Ram
Ravan’s ten heads are the ten pulls of the mind, from lust and anger to pride and ego.
Cut one head and another grows back. Cutting symptoms never ends the problem, because the root is untouched.
नाभिकुंड पियूष बस याकें।
नाथ जिअत रावनु बल ताकें॥
Vibhishan tells Ram: “Lord, the nectar pool in his navel is what keeps Ravan alive and strong.”
Ramcharitmanas · Lanka Kand
The nectar fed Ravan’s Bhram: the delusion that he could never die.
ततो धनुषि संधाय ब्रह्मास्त्रं महाद्युतिः।
सायक एक नाभि सर सोषा। अपर बीस भुज सिर कर कोषा॥
Ram fixed the Brahmastra to his bow and loosed it. The nectar dried up, and with it the Bhram that Ravan could never die.
What the arrow means
A symbolic reading
Ten headsThe vices and pulls of the mind. These are only symptoms.
Amrit in the navelBhram: the delusion of never dying, which feeds ego.
The BrahmastraThe power of truth that dissolves Bhram.
राम = परब्रह्म
Ram is the Parabrahma, the Supreme Reality. Bhram cannot stand before Brahma, so the Brahmastra of truth breaks the delusion of ego.
Parents are the first guru
Kaushalya Mata and Kaikeyi Mata stand behind Ram. The sanskar parents give is the quiet strength behind the arrow that destroys ego.
Happy Dussehra
May the arrow of knowledge destroy the ego within.

The ten heads

  • Kaam: lust
  • Krodha: anger
  • Moha: delusion
  • Lobha: greed
  • Mada: pride
  • Matsarya: envy
  • Manas: the mind
  • Buddhi: the intellect
  • Chitta: the will
  • Ahamkara: ego

What the sources say

Vibhishan’s revelation of the nectar in the navel, and the arrow that dries it, are from Tulsidas’s Ramcharitmanas (Lanka Kand). The Brahmastra is from Valmiki’s Ramayana, where it strikes Ravan’s heart. This film joins the two in a devotional reading. The story of Hanuman taking the special arrow from Mandodari is in neither text and comes from later regional retellings.

The Bhram reading of the Amrit, Ram as Parabrahma, and the scene about parents are interpretations, not lines from the scriptures. Please check the Sanskrit verse against your own edition of the Valmiki Ramayana.
